The cell cycle & mitosis

Before a cell divides it has to copy all of its DNA and then split the copies evenly. Mitosis is the splitting.

The idea

The cycle has two main parts. Interphase (most of the time) includes G₁ (growth), S (DNA is replicated, so each chromosome becomes two identical sister chromatids) and G₂ (more growth and preparation). M phase is mitosis followed by cytokinesis (division of the cytoplasm).

Mitosis has four stages. Prophase: chromosomes condense, the spindle forms and the nuclear envelope breaks down. Metaphase: chromosomes line up at the middle. Anaphase: sister chromatids separate and move to opposite poles. Telophase: nuclei re-form and the chromosomes relax. The two daughter cells are genetically identical to the parent and to each other. Mitosis is used for growth, repair and (in many organisms) asexual reproduction. In animal cells cytokinesis pinches the cell (cleavage furrow); in plant cells a cell plate forms.

Try this

Predict what will happen first. Then set it up and check.

  1. Go to anaphase. What separates, and what does each pole get?

    What you should see

    Sister chromatids separate (each is now a chromosome) and each pole receives a complete set of chromosomes.

  2. Go to S phase. What happens to the DNA amount?

    What you should see

    It doubles (from 2C to 4C) as every chromosome is copied. The chromosome number stays the same until anaphase.

Worked example

In a root tip squash, 200 cells are counted: 160 in interphase, 20 in prophase, 8 in metaphase, 4 in anaphase and 8 in telophase. If the cell cycle takes 24 hours, how long is spent in interphase and in mitosis?

Try it yourself first, then reveal one step at a time.

  1. Fractions. Interphase 160/200 = 0.80; mitosis 40/200 = 0.20.
  2. Interphase time. 0.80 × 24 h = 19.2 h.
  3. Mitosis time. 0.20 × 24 h = 4.8 h.
  4. Assumption. Cells are evenly distributed through the cycle (an asynchronous population).

Common mistakes

  • Saying chromosomes double in number in S phase (the number of chromosomes stays the same; each now has two chromatids).
  • Mixing up the order of anaphase and metaphase.
  • Forgetting cytokinesis is separate from mitosis.
  • Saying daughter cells differ genetically.
  • Thinking DNA replication happens in prophase.

Chromosomes and chromatids

Count chromosomes by centromeres. A cell with 4 chromosomes has 8 chromatids after S phase until anaphase, when each chromatid is counted as a chromosome (so 8 chromosomes briefly). After cytokinesis each daughter has 4 chromosomes and no sister chromatids.
