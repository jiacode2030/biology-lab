Speciation & reproductive isolation

A species is a group whose members can interbreed and produce fertile offspring. Species form when groups stop exchanging genes.

The idea

Under the biological species concept, species are populations that can interbreed in nature and produce viable, fertile offspring and are reproductively isolated from other groups. Isolation can be prezygotic (before a zygote forms): habitat, temporal (breeding times), behavioral (courtship), mechanical (structures do not fit), gametic (sperm and egg incompatible). Or postzygotic (after): reduced hybrid viability, reduced hybrid fertility (mule), hybrid breakdown (later generations weak).

In allopatric speciation a geographic barrier splits a population; each side diverges by selection, drift and mutation until they cannot interbreed. In sympatric speciation new species form in the same area, for example by polyploidy in plants or by strong disruptive selection with mating preferences. Adaptive radiation is rapid diversification from an ancestor into many species filling different niches (Darwin's finches). Where two groups meet, hybrid zones show how complete isolation is.

Try this

Predict what will happen first. Then set it up and check.

  1. Go to the stage where the groups meet again. Why can't they interbreed?

    What you should see

    Changes that built up in isolation (mating signals, timing, chromosome differences) now keep them from producing fertile offspring.

  2. Choose "mule". Is this a prezygotic or postzygotic barrier?

    What you should see

    Postzygotic: horses and donkeys can mate and a hybrid forms, but the hybrid is sterile.

Worked example

Two species of orchids live together and share pollinators. One flowers in May and the other in July, so no hybrids form. Classify the isolation and say what kind of speciation might have produced it.

Try it yourself first, then reveal one step at a time.

  1. Barrier. Different flowering times: temporal isolation, a prezygotic barrier.
  2. Speciation. They share a habitat, so sympatric speciation is plausible (a shift in flowering time creating reproductive isolation).
  3. Evidence. Hybrid formation is prevented before fertilization, so gene flow between them is very low.

Common mistakes

  • Calling a mule's sterility prezygotic.
  • Saying speciation needs a physical barrier.
  • Thinking species form within a single organism's lifetime.
  • Thinking all species concepts give the same answer (asexual organisms and fossils need other concepts).
  • Confusing polyploidy (extra sets of chromosomes) with aneuploidy.

Classification checklist

Ask: does it block mating or fertilization (prezygotic), or does a zygote form but the hybrid fail (postzygotic)? Then ask if populations were apart (allopatric) or together (sympatric).
