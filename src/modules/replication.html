DNA replication

Before a cell divides it copies every base of its DNA. The copy is made by a machine that reads each old strand and builds a new partner.

The idea

Replication is semiconservative: each new DNA molecule has one old strand and one new strand. It starts at an origin of replication. Helicase unwinds the double helix, making a replication fork; single-strand binding proteins keep the strands apart; topoisomerase relieves twisting ahead of the fork. Primase makes a short RNA primer because DNA polymerase can only add nucleotides to an existing 3′ end. DNA polymerase then adds nucleotides only in the 5′ → 3′ direction, reading the template 3′ → 5′.

Because the strands are antiparallel, one new strand (the leading strand) is made continuously toward the fork. The other (the lagging strand) is made away from the fork in short pieces called Okazaki fragments, each needing its own primer. DNA ligase joins the fragments after the primers are replaced with DNA. DNA polymerase also proofreads, so errors are rare (about 1 in 10⁹–10¹⁰ bases after repair).

Try this

Predict what will happen first. Then set it up and check.

  1. Go to the stage with Okazaki fragments. Why does only one strand have them?

    What you should see

    DNA polymerase can only build 5′ → 3′. On one template that direction points toward the fork (continuous); on the other it points away, so the strand is built in pieces as the fork opens.

  2. Set 2 generations. What fraction are hybrid?

    What you should see

    2 of 4 molecules (50%) are hybrid and 2 of 4 are light. No heavy DNA remains, and this matches semiconservative replication.

Worked example

Bacteria grown for many generations in ¹⁵N are moved to ¹⁴N and allowed to divide three times. What fraction of DNA molecules are hybrid and what fraction light?

Try it yourself first, then reveal one step at a time.

  1. Molecules. Each generation doubles the number: 2³ = 8 molecules from one.
  2. Old strands. The two original ¹⁵N strands end up in 2 different molecules, each a hybrid (one old, one new).
  3. Fractions. Hybrid = 2/8 = 1/4; light = 6/8 = 3/4; heavy = 0.

Common mistakes

  • Writing that DNA polymerase works 3′ → 5′ (it adds to a 3′ end, building 5′ → 3′).
  • Thinking primers are DNA. They are short RNA.
  • Saying both strands are made in fragments.
  • Forgetting that the old strands are conserved (one in each daughter).
  • Confusing ligase (joins fragments) with polymerase.

Predicting results

If primase is missing, polymerase cannot start. If ligase is missing, the lagging strand has gaps. If helicase fails, the fork does not open. State which strand is affected and why.
