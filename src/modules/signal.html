Cell signaling

Cells talk to each other with chemical messages. The message never has to enter the cell: the receiving cell converts it into an internal signal.

The idea

Cell communication has three stages. In reception, a signaling molecule (ligand) binds a specific receptor protein, usually on the cell surface (small nonpolar signals such as steroid hormones can cross the membrane and bind receptors inside). In transduction, the receptor changes shape and starts a chain of molecules, often a phosphorylation cascade in which protein kinases activate one another. Second messengers such as cAMP and Ca²⁺ spread the signal quickly. In the response, the cell changes what it does: turns genes on or off, activates an enzyme, or reshapes its cytoskeleton.

Cascades amplify the signal because each activated enzyme activates many molecules in the next step, so a few ligand molecules can trigger a huge response. Signals are specific because only cells with the right receptor respond. Signals act over different distances: direct contact (cell junctions), paracrine (local), synaptic (neurons) and endocrine (hormones in blood). A mutation in any step can block or constantly activate a pathway, which is a common cause of cancer.

Try this

Predict what will happen first. Then set it up and check.

  1. Go to the cascade stage. Why does each step increase the signal?

    What you should see

    Each activated kinase phosphorylates many copies of the next protein, so the number of active molecules multiplies at each step.

  2. Choose "stuck on" with 1 ligand. What happens?

    What you should see

    The pathway runs at full strength with no ligand: the cell responds as if the signal were always present, a typical mechanism of cancer.

Worked example

A signal activates a receptor. The receptor activates 20 kinase A molecules; each kinase A activates 20 kinase B molecules; each kinase B activates 20 enzymes. How many active enzymes result from 1 ligand-bound receptor?

Try it yourself first, then reveal one step at a time.

  1. Step 1. 1 receptor → 20 kinase A.
  2. Step 2. 20 × 20 = 400 kinase B.
  3. Step 3. 400 × 20 = 8,000 active enzymes.
  4. Meaning. One signaling event becomes a response of thousands of molecules: amplification.

Common mistakes

  • Saying the ligand enters the cell and causes the response directly (true only for small nonpolar ligands, and then it binds an internal receptor).
  • Thinking the signal is amplified at reception only.
  • Forgetting that specificity comes from the receptor.
  • Calling a response "transduction". Transduction is the relay.
  • Believing that more ligand always gives a bigger response (receptors can saturate).

Predicting what a mutation does

Name the step affected. If the receptor cannot bind, no response. If a kinase is stuck on, a response without signal. If the cascade is blocked downstream, the ligand binds but nothing happens. Describe the effect on the response and on the cell.

Check yourself: A cell lacks the receptor for a hormone. What is the most likely result when the hormone is present?
