Biotechnology & genetic engineering

Biologists can copy, cut, sort, read and rewrite DNA. Each technique rests on a simple property of DNA.

The idea

PCR (polymerase chain reaction) copies a target region. A cycle has three steps: denature (about 95 °C, strands separate), anneal (about 55 °C, primers bind) and extend (72 °C, a heat-stable polymerase such as Taq builds new strands). Each cycle doubles the target: after n cycles there are about 2n copies. Restriction enzymes cut DNA at specific palindromic sequences. Gel electrophoresis separates DNA fragments by size: DNA is negatively charged and moves toward the positive electrode, and smaller fragments travel farther.

Recombinant DNA: a gene and a plasmid are cut with the same restriction enzyme, joined with ligase, and the plasmid is put into bacteria (transformation); a selectable marker such as antibiotic resistance finds the bacteria that took it up. CRISPR-Cas9 uses a guide RNA to direct the Cas9 enzyme to a matching DNA sequence, where it cuts so the gene can be disrupted or edited. DNA sequencing reads the order of bases. These tools underlie GMOs, gene therapy, forensics and diagnostics.

Try this

Predict what will happen first. Then set it up and check.

  1. Set 10 cycles. How many copies of the target?

    What you should see

    2¹⁰ = 1,024 copies from one starting molecule (in the ideal case).

  2. Digest with enzyme E. Which band travels farthest?

    What you should see

    The smallest fragment (1,500 bp) travels farthest because it moves through the gel mesh most easily.

Worked example

A 6,000 bp linear DNA is cut by enzyme E at positions 1,500 and 4,000. What fragments appear, and which is nearest the wells?

Try it yourself first, then reveal one step at a time.

  1. Cuts. At 1,500 and 4,000: three fragments.
  2. Sizes. 1,500; 4,000 − 1,500 = 2,500; 6,000 − 4,000 = 2,000 bp (sum 6,000).
  3. Gel. The largest (2,500 bp) moves the least, so it is nearest the wells; the 1,500 bp fragment moves farthest.

Common mistakes

  • Saying DNA moves toward the negative electrode (it is negatively charged, so it moves to the positive one).
  • Thinking larger fragments move farther.
  • Forgetting that PCR needs primers and a heat-stable polymerase.
  • Saying every bacterium takes up the plasmid (that is why selection is needed).
  • Believing CRISPR uses protein alone to find the target (the guide RNA does the matching).

Interpreting gels

Compare band positions with the ladder: farther from the wells means smaller. Add up fragment sizes to check they equal the total DNA length. For comparisons (such as a mutation destroying a restriction site), predict how the pattern of bands changes.
