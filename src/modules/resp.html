Cellular respiration

Cells get usable energy by taking sugar apart a little at a time and capturing the energy in ATP.

The idea

Overall: C₆H₁₂O₆ + 6 O₂ → 6 CO₂ + 6 H₂O + energy (ATP and heat). It happens in four steps. Glycolysis (cytoplasm) splits glucose into 2 pyruvate with a net gain of 2 ATP and 2 NADH. Pyruvate oxidation (mitochondrial matrix) converts each pyruvate to acetyl-CoA, releasing CO₂ and making NADH. The citric acid cycle (matrix) completes the breakdown, releasing CO₂ and making ATP, NADH and FADH₂. The electron transport chain (inner membrane) takes electrons from NADH and FADH₂, pumps H⁺ across the membrane and uses O₂ as the final electron acceptor (forming water).

The resulting H⁺ gradient drives ATP synthase (chemiosmosis), which makes most of the ATP: roughly 30–32 per glucose in all. Without oxygen the chain stops, NADH cannot be recycled, and the cell must use fermentation instead.

Worked example

Starting from 1 glucose, how many CO₂ are made in glycolysis, pyruvate oxidation and the citric acid cycle, and where does the electron transport chain get its electrons?

Try it yourself first, then reveal one step at a time.

  1. Glycolysis. No CO₂ (glucose is split into two 3-carbon pyruvates).
  2. Pyruvate oxidation. 1 CO₂ per pyruvate, so 2.
  3. Citric acid cycle. 2 CO₂ per turn, 2 turns per glucose: 4. Total 6, matching the 6 carbons of glucose.
  4. Electrons. From NADH and FADH₂ made in the earlier steps.

Common mistakes

  • Thinking CO₂ is released in the electron transport chain. It comes from pyruvate oxidation and the citric acid cycle.
  • Putting glycolysis in the mitochondrion.
  • Saying oxygen is used up in glycolysis.
  • Confusing the H⁺ gradient (made by the chain) with ATP (made by ATP synthase).
  • Memorizing 36 ATP; the commonly accepted figure is about 30–32.

Predict and explain

If a poison blocks the electron transport chain, NADH and FADH₂ accumulate, the H⁺ gradient collapses, ATP production falls sharply, and the cell can only run glycolysis. Questions often ask what happens to O₂ use, ATP and NADH levels: reason along the pathway.
