Surface area to volume

A cell must take in nutrients through its surface but needs them for its whole volume. As a cell grows, the volume outpaces the surface, and that limits cell size.

The idea

For a cube of side s: surface area = 6s², volume = s³, so SA:V = 6/s. For a sphere of radius r: SA = 4πr², V = (4/3)πr³, so SA:V = 3/r. In both cases SA:V decreases as the cell gets bigger. Doubling the side multiplies SA by 4 but volume by 8.

This explains why cells are small, why gas exchange organs (lungs, gills, root hairs, villi) are folded or branched to add surface area, and why large organisms need transport systems. A cell of any shape exchanges materials faster when its SA:V is higher, so a flattened or elongated cell does better than a sphere of the same volume.

Try this

Predict what will happen first. Then set it up and check.

  1. Double the size from 2 to 4 (cube). What happens to SA, V and SA:V?

    What you should see

    SA goes 24 → 96 (×4), V goes 8 → 64 (×8), so SA:V falls from 3 to 1.5 (÷2).

  2. Switch to a sphere at radius 3. Compare its SA:V with a cube of side 3.

    What you should see

    Sphere: 3/3 = 1.0; cube side 3: 6/3 = 2.0. They are measured differently: the sphere of radius 3 is much bigger than a cube of side 3.

Worked example

Cell 1 is a cube of side 2 µm and Cell 2 is a cube of side 4 µm. Which is better at exchanging materials and by what factor?

Try it yourself first, then reveal one step at a time.

  1. Cell 1. SA = 6 × 2² = 24 µm², V = 8 µm³, SA:V = 3.
  2. Cell 2. SA = 6 × 4² = 96 µm², V = 64 µm³, SA:V = 1.5.
  3. Compare. Cell 1 has twice the SA:V, so it is better at exchange (the smaller cell).

Common mistakes

  • Concluding that a bigger cell has more surface area per unit volume.
  • Mixing up SA:V with total surface area.
  • Applying 6/s to a sphere.
  • Forgetting units: µm², µm³ and µm⁻¹.
  • Thinking cells fix the problem by getting bigger. Folding, flattening or branching raises SA:V instead.

Applying SA:V on the exam

A typical FRQ gives dimensions, asks for SA:V of two cells, then asks which is better at exchange or how an adaptation (microvilli, root hairs, branching) helps. Show the calculation, compare the ratios, and tie it to diffusion distance and exchange rate.
