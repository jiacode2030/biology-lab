Genetic drift & gene flow

In small populations, chance alone can change allele frequencies, and it can even remove an allele altogether.

The idea

Genetic drift is random change in allele frequencies from one generation to the next, because a small sample of alleles is passed on. The smaller the population, the larger the effect; in a small population an allele can be lost or fixed (reach 100%) by chance within a few generations. Drift reduces genetic variation and does not favor adaptive alleles.

Two common cases: the bottleneck effect (a disaster leaves only a few survivors, and the survivors' alleles are not representative; for example northern elephant seals) and the founder effect (a few individuals start a new population with a non-random sample of alleles). Gene flow (migration of alleles between populations) makes populations more similar. Mutation adds new variation; selection, drift, gene flow and mutation together drive evolution.

Worked example

A population of 10,000 beetles is 50% green and 50% brown (neutral trait). A flood leaves 10 survivors, 8 green and 2 brown, and the population regrows. Predict the allele frequencies and name the process.

Try it yourself first, then reveal one step at a time.

  1. After the bottleneck. 8/10 = 80% green, 20% brown (if the trait is a single gene with simple alleles, roughly).
  2. Process. A bottleneck effect: genetic drift in a very small sample.
  3. Consequence. The regrown population is mostly green even though green was no better, and overall genetic variation is lower.

Common mistakes

  • Saying drift is a response to the environment.
  • Saying drift is strong in large populations.
  • Mixing up bottleneck (a population shrinks) and founder effect (a few individuals start a new population).
  • Thinking drift always increases fitness.
  • Forgetting that gene flow brings alleles into a population.

Which force is it?

Ask: Is the change random and size-dependent? Drift. Does it favor traits that raise reproduction? Selection. Are individuals moving between populations? Gene flow. Are new alleles appearing? Mutation.
