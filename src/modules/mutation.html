Mutations

A change in the DNA sequence may change nothing, make a protein slightly different, or ruin it completely. What happens depends on the kind of change and where it occurs.

The idea

A point mutation changes one base. A silent mutation changes the codon but not the amino acid (the code is redundant). A missense mutation changes one amino acid (sickle cell: GAG → GUG, glutamic acid → valine). A nonsense mutation turns an amino acid codon into a stop codon, so the protein is cut short. An insertion or deletion of bases that is not a multiple of three causes a frameshift: every codon after it is read differently, usually ruining the protein. Insertions or deletions of multiples of three add or remove amino acids without shifting the frame.

Mutations arise from copying errors, mutagens (UV, radiation, chemicals) and mistakes in repair. They create new alleles, the raw material for evolution. Only mutations in germ cells (gametes) are passed to offspring; somatic mutations can cause cancer. Many mutations are neutral, some harmful, and a few beneficial depending on the environment.

Try this

Predict what will happen first. Then set it up and check.

  1. Choose missense. Which codon changed and what is the result?

    What you should see

    GAA → GUA changes Glu to Val: one amino acid differs, the rest of the protein is the same.

  2. Choose "insert 1 base". Compare with "delete 3 bases".

    What you should see

    One base shifts the reading frame so all later codons change (frameshift). Deleting exactly three bases removes one amino acid and leaves the frame intact.

Worked example

The coding strand reads 5′-ATG GCT GAA AAG TTT GGA TAA-3′. A mutation changes AAG to TAG. Name the mutation and predict the protein.

Try it yourself first, then reveal one step at a time.

  1. Original. Met–Ala–Glu–Lys–Phe–Gly (stop).
  2. Change. AAG → TAG: in mRNA AAG (Lys) becomes UAG, a stop codon.
  3. Type and effect. Nonsense. The protein is Met–Ala–Glu (3 amino acids), likely nonfunctional.

Common mistakes

  • Calling all mutations harmful.
  • Saying a silent mutation does not change the DNA (it does, but not the amino acid).
  • Forgetting that a frameshift needs an insertion or deletion not divisible by 3.
  • Thinking somatic mutations are inherited.
  • Mixing nonsense (stop) with missense (different amino acid).

Classify by comparing

Translate the original and the mutant side by side. Same amino acids: silent. One different: missense. Stop appears early: nonsense. Many different after a point: frameshift. Remember the effect on function depends on where the change is.
