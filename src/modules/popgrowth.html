Population growth

A population grows quickly when resources are plentiful and levels off when they run short. The shape of the curve says what is limiting it.

The idea

If resources are unlimited, a population grows exponentially: the growth rate is proportional to the number of individuals, dN/dt = rmax N, where r is the per capita growth rate (births minus deaths per individual). The curve is J-shaped and gets steeper as N rises. Real populations eventually face limits, so growth follows the logistic model: dN/dt = rmax N (K − N)/K, with carrying capacity K the largest population the environment can sustain. Growth is fastest at N = K/2 and falls to zero at K.

Density-dependent factors (competition, predation, disease, waste) slow growth more as the population gets crowded. Density-independent factors (fire, storms, freezes) affect a population regardless of its size. Species with high r and many small offspring that get little care are r-selected; species with low r, few offspring and heavy parental care are K-selected. Survivorship curves show how many individuals survive with age: Type I (most live long, die old: humans), Type II (constant death rate: many birds), Type III (high early death: oysters, most plants).

Try this

Predict what will happen first. Then set it up and check.

  1. Choose logistic with K = 1000. At what population is growth fastest?

    What you should see

    At N = K/2 = 500, where (K − N)/K and N are balanced. Past that, growth slows as resources run out.

  2. Switch to exponential. What happens to the curve?

    What you should see

    It keeps getting steeper (J-shaped) with no limit, which cannot continue for long in nature.

Worked example

A population of 200 deer in a reserve has rmax = 0.3 per year and K = 1,000. Find dN/dt at N = 200 and at N = 500.

Try it yourself first, then reveal one step at a time.

  1. Formula. dN/dt = r N (K − N)/K.
  2. At N = 200. 0.3 × 200 × (800/1000) = 48 deer per year.
  3. At N = 500. 0.3 × 500 × (500/1000) = 75 deer per year, the maximum.

Common mistakes

  • Saying logistic growth is fastest at K.
  • Using r as the total growth rather than per capita.
  • Forgetting density-dependent factors act more strongly at high density.
  • Thinking carrying capacity never changes.
  • Confusing survivorship types with growth types.

Using the equations

Substitute the values into the formula from the sheet and keep units (individuals per year). For graph questions: a J-shape means unlimited resources; an S-shape that levels at K means limited resources. Explain the limit with a density-dependent factor (food, space, disease).
