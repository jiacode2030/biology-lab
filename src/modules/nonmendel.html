Beyond simple dominance

Not every trait is a simple dominant-recessive pair. Alleles can blend, share, or be hidden by other genes.

The idea

Incomplete dominance: the heterozygote is intermediate (red × white snapdragons give pink; a 1 : 2 : 1 phenotype ratio in the next generation). Codominance: both alleles are fully expressed (AB blood type; a roan coat with both red and white hairs). Multiple alleles: a population can have more than two alleles of a gene (ABO blood: IA, IB and i; A and B are codominant, i is recessive).

Polygenic traits depend on many genes and show a continuous range (height, skin colour) that forms a bell-shaped curve. Epistasis: one gene masks the expression of another (in labrador coat colour the gene for pigment deposition can hide the gene for pigment colour, giving 9 : 3 : 4). The environment also affects phenotype (hydrangea colour depends on soil pH). These patterns still follow Mendel's rules for how alleles are inherited; they differ in how alleles combine to make the phenotype.

Try this

Predict what will happen first. Then set it up and check.

  1. Choose incomplete dominance. What fraction of the offspring look like the heterozygote?

    What you should see

    1/2 (the Aa genotype), and phenotypes are 1 : 2 : 1 instead of 3 : 1.

  2. Choose epistasis. What ratio appears?

    What you should see

    9 : 3 : 4. The 3 : 1 class and the 1 class that carry the masking genotype look identical (here, yellow).

  3. Cross IᴬIᴮ with ii. What types can the children have?

    What you should see

    Half type A (Iᴬi) and half type B (Iᴮi). No child is AB or O.

Worked example

A man with blood type A (his mother was type O) and a woman with type B (her father was type O) have children. What are the parents' genotypes and what types are possible?

Try it yourself first, then reveal one step at a time.

  1. Man. Type A, and he inherited i from his type O mother, so he is IAi.
  2. Woman. Type B, and she inherited i from her type O father, so she is IBi.
  3. Cross. IAi × IBi gives IAIB (AB), IAi (A), IBi (B) and ii (O), each 1/4. All four types are possible.

Common mistakes

  • Calling incomplete dominance a "blend that cannot separate again". The alleles are still inherited separately, and red and white reappear in the next generation.
  • Thinking codominance and incomplete dominance are the same. In codominance both alleles show fully (distinct); in incomplete dominance the result is intermediate.
  • Saying a person has three ABO alleles. Each person has two; the population has three.
  • Thinking polygenic traits break Mendel's laws.
  • Missing that epistasis changes the 9 : 3 : 3 : 1 ratio.

Matching ratios to patterns

3 : 1 → simple dominance. 1 : 2 : 1 → incomplete dominance or codominance from a heterozygote cross. 9 : 3 : 4 (or 9 : 7, 12 : 3 : 1) → epistasis. A continuous range with a bell curve → polygenic with environment effects.
