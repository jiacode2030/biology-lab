Hardy–Weinberg equilibrium

If nothing is changing allele frequencies, genotype frequencies follow a simple pattern. Departures from that pattern tell you evolution is happening.

The idea

For a gene with two alleles, let p be the frequency of the dominant allele and q the frequency of the recessive allele: p + q = 1. If the population is in Hardy–Weinberg equilibrium, the genotype frequencies after random mating are p² + 2pq + q² = 1 (p² homozygous dominant, 2pq heterozygous, q² homozygous recessive). Allele frequencies do not change from generation to generation.

Equilibrium needs five conditions: very large population (no drift), random mating, no mutation, no gene flow, and no natural selection. Real populations violate them, so the equation is a null model: if observed genotype frequencies differ from expected (tested with chi-square), at least one condition is violated. Typical use: given the frequency of the recessive phenotype (q²), find q = √(q²), then p = 1 − q and the carrier frequency 2pq.

Try this

Predict what will happen first. Then set it up and check.

  1. Set q = 0.2. What is the carrier frequency?

    What you should see

    p = 0.8; 2pq = 2(0.8)(0.2) = 0.32, so 32% of the population are carriers; only 4% (q²) show the recessive phenotype.

  2. Observed counts 360, 480, 160 are what Hardy–Weinberg predicts for q = 0.4 (1000 individuals). Now try 500, 200, 300.

    What you should see

    Far too few heterozygotes: χ² is large, so the population is not in equilibrium (for example, inbreeding or selection).

Worked example

In a population in Hardy–Weinberg equilibrium, 4% of people show a recessive trait. What is the frequency of carriers?

Try it yourself first, then reveal one step at a time.

  1. q². q² = 0.04, so q = √0.04 = 0.2.
  2. p. p = 1 − 0.2 = 0.8.
  3. Carriers. 2pq = 2 × 0.8 × 0.2 = 0.32.
  4. Answer. 32% carry the allele without showing the trait.

Common mistakes

  • Setting q² = frequency of the recessive allele (q² is the frequency of the genotype, q is the allele).
  • Using the carrier frequency as q.
  • Forgetting that the equation only applies when the five conditions hold.
  • Saying 2pq is homozygous.
  • Adding p and q to different totals (counts instead of frequencies).

Common question types

(1) Given q², find q, p, 2pq. (2) Given genotype counts, find allele frequencies p = (2·AA + Aa)/(2N). (3) Given observed vs expected, use chi-square (df = 1) to judge equilibrium. (4) Say which condition is violated when allele frequencies change.
