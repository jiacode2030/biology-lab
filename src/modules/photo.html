Photosynthesis

Plants make sugar from air and water, and the energy for it comes from light. The process has two linked stages: one that captures light energy and one that spends it to build sugar.

The idea

In the light reactions (thylakoid membranes), light excites electrons in photosystems. Water is split to replace them, releasing O₂. The electrons pass along an electron transport chain, pumping H⁺ into the thylakoid space. The H⁺ gradient drives ATP synthase (chemiosmosis) to make ATP, and the electrons end up in NADPH.

In the Calvin cycle (stroma), the enzyme rubisco attaches CO₂ to RuBP (carbon fixation). ATP and NADPH from the light reactions reduce the product to G3P, a three-carbon sugar. Some G3P leaves to make glucose and the rest regenerates RuBP. Overall: 6 CO₂ + 6 H₂O + light → C₆H₁₂O₆ + 6 O₂. The oxygen comes from the water.

Try this

Predict what will happen first. Then set it up and check.

  1. Go to the stage where the H⁺ gradient is used. What is made?

    What you should see

    ATP: H⁺ flows through ATP synthase from the thylakoid space into the stroma, driving phosphorylation of ADP.

  2. Set light to 90 and CO₂ to 10. What limits the rate?

    What you should see

    CO₂: the Calvin cycle is short of substrate, so more light does not help.

  3. Set temperature to 45 °C with plenty of light and CO₂. Why does the rate drop?

    What you should see

    High temperature denatures enzymes (like rubisco), so the rate falls even though light and CO₂ are plentiful.

Worked example

How many ATP and NADPH are needed for the Calvin cycle to make one molecule of glucose (6 CO₂ fixed)?

Try it yourself first, then reveal one step at a time.

  1. Per 3 CO₂. Making one G3P takes 9 ATP and 6 NADPH.
  2. Glucose needs 6 CO₂. That is two rounds of 3 CO₂, making 2 G3P.
  3. Totals. 2 × 9 = 18 ATP and 2 × 6 = 12 NADPH.

Common mistakes

  • Saying the oxygen released comes from CO₂. It comes from water.
  • Saying the Calvin cycle needs no light at all. It depends on ATP and NADPH made in the light reactions.
  • Putting the light reactions in the stroma and the Calvin cycle in the thylakoid.
  • Mixing up photosynthesis and respiration electron carriers (NADPH vs NADH).
  • Forgetting that the light reactions also need a source of electrons: water.

Experiment questions

Predict the trend, name the limiting factor at each region of the graph, and explain it using the stage affected (light reactions for light; Calvin cycle for CO₂ and temperature-sensitive enzymes).
