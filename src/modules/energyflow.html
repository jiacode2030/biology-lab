Energy flow & trophic levels

Energy enters ecosystems as sunlight and leaves as heat. Only a small part of it passes from one feeding level to the next.

The idea

Producers (autotrophs) capture light and make organic matter; consumers (heterotrophs) eat it; decomposers break down dead material and return nutrients. Each feeding step is a trophic level. At each step most energy is lost, mainly as heat from cell respiration and in material that is not eaten or digested; typically only about 10% of the energy in one level is stored in the next. So energy pyramids narrow quickly and food chains rarely have more than four or five levels.

Gross primary productivity (GPP) is the total energy captured by producers; net primary productivity (NPP) = GPP − R (R is the producers' respiration) is what is available to consumers. Unlike energy, nutrients cycle. A food web shows many interconnected food chains. Biomass and numbers can also be shown in pyramids, though these can be inverted (for example a pyramid of numbers with one tree and many insects).

Try this

Predict what will happen first. Then set it up and check.

  1. Use 50,000 kJ at producers and 10% efficiency. How much reaches secondary consumers?

    What you should see

    5,000 kJ reaches primary consumers and 500 kJ reaches secondary consumers.

  2. Raise the efficiency to 20%. What changes?

    What you should see

    Each level holds twice as much as before, so the top levels get far more (4 times at the second step, 8 times at the third), and the food chain could support more levels.

Worked example

A grassland produces 20,000 kJ of NPP per m² per year. If 10% passes to each higher level, how much energy do secondary consumers (level 3) get?

Try it yourself first, then reveal one step at a time.

  1. Producers. 20,000 kJ.
  2. Primary consumers. 10% × 20,000 = 2,000 kJ.
  3. Secondary consumers. 10% × 2,000 = 200 kJ.

Common mistakes

  • Saying 90% of energy is "used up". It is lost mainly as heat and in unconsumed or undigested matter.
  • Thinking energy cycles (it does not).
  • Starting the pyramid with the wrong level.
  • Mixing GPP and NPP.
  • Saying decomposers are not part of energy flow.

Explaining the limit on chain length

Energy declines by about 90% per level, so after four or five levels there is too little left to support a population. Mention heat loss from cellular respiration and uneaten matter.
