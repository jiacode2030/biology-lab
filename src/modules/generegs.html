Regulation of gene expression

Every cell has the same genes but uses different ones. Cells switch genes on and off depending on conditions and identity.

The idea

In bacteria, genes with related functions are grouped in an operon with one promoter and an operator. In the lac operon (inducible), a repressor normally blocks the operator so the genes for lactose digestion are off. If lactose is present, a derivative binds the repressor and releases it, and transcription can happen. When glucose is also present the CAP–cAMP activator is not active, so transcription is low even with lactose. In the trp operon (repressible), tryptophan binds the repressor and turns it on, so the genes are switched off when tryptophan is plentiful.

In eukaryotes there are more control points: chromatin structure (acetylation of histones loosens DNA; DNA methylation silences genes: epigenetics), transcription factors binding enhancers and promoters, alternative splicing, mRNA stability and microRNAs (RNA interference), and control of translation and protein breakdown. This is how identical DNA yields different cell types.

Try this

Predict what will happen first. Then set it up and check.

  1. Lactose present and glucose absent. What happens?

    What you should see

    The repressor is released and the CAP–cAMP activator is active: high transcription of the lac genes.

  2. Now add glucose back with lactose present.

    What you should see

    Transcription drops: cAMP is low so CAP does not activate, even though the repressor is off. The cell prefers glucose.

  3. Set tryptophan high. Why are the trp genes off?

    What you should see

    Tryptophan binds the repressor and activates it, so it blocks the operator: no need to make more tryptophan.

Worked example

E. coli has a mutation so that the lac repressor cannot bind lactose (but still binds the operator). Predict lac gene expression with and without lactose.

Try it yourself first, then reveal one step at a time.

  1. Normal repressor. Releases from the operator when lactose is present.
  2. Mutant. It cannot respond to lactose, so it stays on the operator.
  3. Result. The genes stay off with or without lactose. The cell cannot digest lactose.

Common mistakes

  • Saying lactose binds the operator (it binds the repressor, via a derivative).
  • Mixing up inducible (lac, normally off) and repressible (trp, normally on) operons.
  • Saying operons are found in eukaryotes (they are typical of prokaryotes).
  • Assuming methylation always turns genes on (it usually silences them).
  • Forgetting that every cell has the same DNA; the difference is in gene expression.
