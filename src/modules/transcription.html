Transcription & RNA processing

DNA stays in the nucleus; a working copy of one gene travels out. That copy is made of RNA.

The idea

Transcription makes an RNA copy of a gene. RNA polymerase binds to the promoter, unwinds a short stretch of DNA and builds an RNA strand 5′ → 3′ by pairing RNA nucleotides with the template strand (A–U, T–A, G–C, C–G). No primer is needed. The RNA is complementary to the template and has the same sequence as the coding strand, with U in place of T. Transcription ends at a terminator sequence.

In prokaryotes the RNA is used immediately. In eukaryotes the primary transcript is processed before leaving the nucleus: a 5′ cap and a poly-A tail are added, and introns are removed while exons are joined (splicing) to make mature mRNA. Alternative splicing lets one gene make several proteins. Other RNAs (tRNA, rRNA) are also made by transcription and are not translated.

Try this

Predict what will happen first. Then set it up and check.

  1. Go to the processing stage. What is removed and what is added?

    What you should see

    Introns are removed and exons joined (splicing); a 5′ cap and a poly-A tail are added.

  2. The template reads 3′-TACGGATTC-5′. What is the mRNA?

    What you should see

    5′-AUGCCUAAG-3′: each base is replaced by its RNA partner and the strand runs the other way.

Worked example

A template strand reads 3′-TACCGATTT-5′. Write the mRNA and the coding strand.

Try it yourself first, then reveal one step at a time.

  1. Pair bases. T→A, A→U, C→G, C→G, G→C, A→U, T→A, T→A, T→A: AUGGCUAAA.
  2. mRNA. 5′-AUGGCUAAA-3′.
  3. Coding strand. It has the same sequence with T for U: 5′-ATGGCTAAA-3′.

Common mistakes

  • Writing T in mRNA.
  • Copying the template instead of its complement.
  • Reading the template in the wrong direction (the RNA is built 5′ → 3′, antiparallel to the template).
  • Saying introns are translated.
  • Confusing the template strand with the coding strand.

Common question types

Given a template or coding strand, write the mRNA and then the peptide. Given a mutation in the promoter or a splice site, predict the effect: no transcription, or the wrong exons joined.
