Cell organelles & compartments

A eukaryotic cell is a set of rooms, each with its own job. Keeping reactions in separate compartments is what lets a cell run many incompatible processes at once.

The idea

The nucleus holds the DNA. Ribosomes (not membrane-bound) build proteins; those on the rough ER make proteins for export or membranes, and the smooth ER makes lipids and detoxifies. The Golgi modifies, sorts and packages proteins into vesicles. Mitochondria carry out most cellular respiration and have a double membrane with folded inner cristae. Lysosomes digest material with hydrolytic enzymes. Plant cells also have chloroplasts (photosynthesis), a large central vacuole and a cell wall.

Compartments matter because each has its own conditions. Lysosome enzymes work at acidic pH that would damage the rest of the cell; the inner mitochondrial membrane holds a proton gradient. Prokaryotes have no membrane-bound organelles, but they do have ribosomes, a plasma membrane, DNA in a nucleoid and (usually) a cell wall.

Try this

Predict what will happen first. Then set it up and check.

  1. Pick the mitochondrion. Why does it have two membranes?

    What you should see

    The double membrane (with the inner one folded into cristae) creates two compartments and a large surface for the electron transport chain; it also reflects its origin as an engulfed bacterium.

  2. Step through the secretion pathway to the Golgi. What happens there?

    What you should see

    The protein is modified (for example by adding carbohydrate), sorted and packaged into a vesicle that carries it to the plasma membrane.

Worked example

Insulin is a protein secreted by pancreas cells. List the organelles it passes through in order.

Try it yourself first, then reveal one step at a time.

  1. Made. Translated by a ribosome on the rough ER; the growing chain enters the ER lumen.
  2. Transported. A transport vesicle buds off the ER and fuses with the Golgi.
  3. Processed and packaged. The Golgi modifies it and packages it in a secretory vesicle.
  4. Secreted. The vesicle fuses with the plasma membrane and releases insulin by exocytosis.

Common mistakes

  • Saying ribosomes are membrane-bound organelles.
  • Thinking the Golgi makes proteins. It processes and sorts them.
  • Mixing up smooth ER (lipids, detox) and rough ER (ribosomes, protein).
  • Believing prokaryotes have no ribosomes. They do, just not membrane-bound organelles.
  • Putting chloroplasts in animal cells.

Why compartments help

Compartments increase efficiency by concentrating enzymes and substrates, and protect the cell from hazardous reactions. Membranes inside organelles add surface area for reactions: cristae in mitochondria, thylakoids in chloroplasts, folded ER.
