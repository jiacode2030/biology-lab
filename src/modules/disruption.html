Human impact & ecosystem disruption

Ecosystems can absorb some disturbance, but human activity often pushes them past what they can recover from.

The idea

Eutrophication: fertilizer or sewage adds nitrogen and phosphorus to a lake; algae bloom; when they die, decomposers use up the oxygen, and fish and other animals suffocate. Biomagnification: toxins that are fat-soluble or not broken down (DDT, mercury, PCBs) become more concentrated at each trophic level, so top predators are most affected. Invasive species can outcompete natives. Habitat fragmentation splits populations and reduces gene flow. Climate change shifts ranges and timing of life cycles, and ocean acidification harms organisms with calcium carbonate shells.

Biodiversity matters for ecosystem stability and services. Ecosystems with more species and more genetic diversity tend to be more resilient. Conservation tools include protected areas and corridors, restoring habitat, reducing pollution, controlling invasives and sustainable harvesting. Keystone species and indicator species help monitor ecosystem health.

Try this

Predict what will happen first. Then set it up and check.

  1. Set the water at 0.01 ppb and a factor of 10. How concentrated is it in the bird?

    What you should see

    0.01 × 10⁵ = 1,000 ppb: a hundred thousand times higher than in the water.

  2. Go to the stage when algae die. Where does the oxygen go?

    What you should see

    Decomposers (bacteria) use it up as they break down the dead algae by aerobic respiration.

Worked example

A pesticide is 0.5 ppb in lake water. Each trophic level concentrates it 10-fold. Algae take it up from water and are eaten by zooplankton, then small fish, then a heron. What is the concentration in the heron?

Try it yourself first, then reveal one step at a time.

  1. Algae. 0.5 × 10 = 5 ppb.
  2. Zooplankton. 50 ppb.
  3. Small fish. 500 ppb.
  4. Heron. 5,000 ppb.

Common mistakes

  • Saying toxins are diluted as they go up the food chain.
  • Mixing up eutrophication (nutrient overload) and biomagnification (toxin accumulation).
  • Saying algae blooms add oxygen to the lake when they die.
  • Thinking invasive species are always harmful in their home range.
  • Forgetting that fragmentation reduces gene flow.
