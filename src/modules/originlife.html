Origin of life & evidence from the past

Life on Earth began almost 4 billion years ago, from chemistry that did not need life to start.

The idea

The early Earth (about 4.6 billion years ago) had little or no free oxygen and was bombarded with energy. In the Miller–Urey experiment (1953), a mixture of water vapor, methane, ammonia and hydrogen exposed to sparks produced amino acids, showing that organic molecules can form abiotically. Hypotheses: the RNA world (RNA can store information and act as a catalyst, so it may have come before DNA and proteins) and protocells (lipid vesicles with simple metabolism that could grow and divide). The oldest evidence of life is about 3.5 billion years old (stromatolites: layered rocks made by prokaryotes).

Photosynthetic cyanobacteria raised oxygen in the atmosphere about 2.4 billion years ago, which changed Earth's chemistry and living things. Eukaryotes appear about 2 billion years ago (endosymbiosis), multicellular organisms later, and most animal groups in the Cambrian about 540 million years ago. We know the timeline from fossils and radiometric dating: radioactive isotopes decay at a constant half-life, so the fraction remaining gives the age. Age = (number of half-lives) × (half-life).

Try this

Predict what will happen first. Then set it up and check.

  1. Set 25% remaining for carbon-14. How old is the sample?

    What you should see

    25% = (½)², so 2 half-lives = 11,460 years.

  2. Switch to uranium-238 at 50%. What age?

    What you should see

    One half-life, about 4.47 billion years: uranium dates very old rocks, not recent fossils.

  3. Set the timeline to 2.4 billion years ago.

    What you should see

    Photosynthetic cyanobacteria are raising atmospheric oxygen; life is still single-celled.

Worked example

A fossil contains 12.5% of its original carbon-14. (Half-life 5,730 years.) How old is it?

Try it yourself first, then reveal one step at a time.

  1. Fraction. 12.5% = 1/8 = (½)³.
  2. Half-lives. 3 half-lives have passed.
  3. Age. 3 × 5,730 = 17,190 years.

Common mistakes

  • Using carbon-14 for rocks millions of years old (too little remains).
  • Mixing up the number of half-lives and the age.
  • Saying Miller–Urey made life (it made amino acids).
  • Saying the RNA world is proven (it is a hypothesis with supporting evidence).
  • Putting eukaryotes before the first prokaryotes.

Reading evidence

Answers should connect the evidence to the claim: fossils and radiometric dates give timing; Miller–Urey shows monomers can form; ribozymes support an RNA world; shared genes and the universal code support common ancestry.
