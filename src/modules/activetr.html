Active transport & bulk transport

Moving a substance down its gradient is free. Moving it up the gradient costs energy, and cells spend a lot of ATP doing it.

The idea

Facilitated diffusion uses channel or carrier proteins but still moves substances down their concentration gradient: no energy needed. Active transport uses energy (usually ATP) and a protein pump to move a substance against its gradient. The sodium-potassium pump moves 3 Na⁺ out and 2 K⁺ in per ATP, keeping Na⁺ low and K⁺ high inside the cell. Cotransport uses the gradient built by a pump to move a second substance (for example glucose with Na⁺).

Large molecules and particles move by vesicles: endocytosis brings material in (phagocytosis for particles, receptor-mediated for specific molecules) and exocytosis sends it out. These also use energy.

Try this

Predict what will happen first. Then set it up and check.

  1. Run the pump lab to the stage where ATP is used. What does it do?

    What you should see

    ATP transfers a phosphate to the pump, changing its shape so that the sodium binding site faces outside.

  2. Choose facilitated diffusion and run it, then try the active pump with ATP off. What differs?

    What you should see

    Facilitated diffusion evens out the two sides without ATP. The pump moves particles to the side that already has more only when ATP is on; with ATP off it does nothing.

Worked example

A cell has 10 mM K⁺ outside and 140 mM inside. K⁺ moves in through the Na⁺/K⁺ pump. Is this passive or active? Explain.

Try it yourself first, then reveal one step at a time.

  1. Gradient. K⁺ is higher inside (140) than outside (10).
  2. Direction. It moves in, toward the higher concentration: against the gradient.
  3. Conclusion. Movement against a gradient requires energy: this is active transport using ATP.

Common mistakes

  • Thinking facilitated diffusion uses ATP.
  • Saying active transport always moves substances in. It can move them out (Na⁺).
  • Mixing up the 3 and 2 in the sodium-potassium pump.
  • Forgetting that vesicle transport also requires energy.
  • Saying channel proteins pump ions. They let ions through down a gradient.

Spotting the type

Ask: which way relative to the gradient, and does it need a protein or ATP? Down the gradient and no protein: simple diffusion. Down the gradient with a protein: facilitated diffusion. Up the gradient: active transport. Large particle in a vesicle: endo- or exocytosis.
