Linked genes & recombination

Genes on the same chromosome tend to travel together. How often they separate tells you how far apart they are.

The idea

Independent assortment only applies to genes on different chromosomes. Genes close together on the same chromosome are linked: they are inherited together unless crossing over between them breaks the pairing. The offspring with the original (parental) combinations are parental types; those with new combinations are recombinants.

The recombination frequency = recombinants / total offspring × 100%. The farther apart two genes, the more often a crossover falls between them. By definition 1% recombination = 1 map unit (centimorgan). Recombination frequencies cannot exceed 50%; at 50% the genes behave as if unlinked. Distances from three genes let you order them on a chromosome. A test cross (AaBb × aabb) makes recombinants easy to count because the recessive parent contributes only recessive alleles.

Try this

Predict what will happen first. Then set it up and check.

  1. Set the distance to 12 map units. What fraction of offspring are recombinants?

    What you should see

    About 12% (120 of 1000), split evenly between the two recombinant types.

  2. Set the distance to 50. What do you notice?

    What you should see

    Half the offspring are recombinants: the four classes are equal, just like independent assortment.

Worked example

In a test cross, 1,000 offspring are produced: 440 AB, 440 ab, 60 Ab and 60 aB. Find the distance between the genes.

Try it yourself first, then reveal one step at a time.

  1. Recombinants. Ab and aB are recombinants: 60 + 60 = 120.
  2. Frequency. 120/1000 = 0.12 = 12%.
  3. Map distance. 12 map units.

Common mistakes

  • Counting parental types as recombinants.
  • Forgetting that the recombination frequency cannot exceed 50%.
  • Using the wrong total.
  • Saying genes at 50% recombination are on different chromosomes (they could be far apart on one).
  • Adding map distances without checking the order.

Mapping three genes

The two most distant genes have the largest recombination frequency, and it is close to the sum of the other two: A–B 10, B–C 6, A–C 16 means order A, B, C. Check that the largest equals the sum of the other two (approximately).

Check yourself: Two genes show 5% recombination. How far apart are they?
