Rates, Q10 & dilutions

Biology is full of "how fast": how fast a reaction runs, how fast a population grows, how diluted a solution is. A few formulas cover most of it.

The idea

A rate is a change per unit time: rate = Δquantity / Δtime. On a graph of quantity against time, the rate is the slope. To compare conditions, measure the same change over the same time.

The temperature coefficient Q10 says how much a rate changes for a 10 °C rise: Q10 = (R2/R1)10/(T2 − T1). Many biological rates have Q10 near 2. This only holds below the temperature that denatures the enzymes. A serial dilution makes a solution weaker in equal steps: each step multiplies the dilution by the same factor.

Try this

Predict what will happen first. Then set it up and check.

  1. Set Q10 to 2 and the temperature to 30 °C. By how much did the rate change from 20 °C?

    What you should see

    It doubled (10 → 20). That is what Q10 = 2 means for a 10 °C rise.

  2. Make a 1 : 10 dilution series starting at 80 mg/mL. What is tube 3?

    What you should see

    80 / 10³ = 0.08 mg/mL. Every tube is a tenth of the one before.

Worked example

A reaction runs at 6 µmol/min at 20 °C and 15 µmol/min at 35 °C. Find Q10 and estimate the rate at 25 °C.

Try it yourself first, then reveal one step at a time.

  1. Setup. R1 = 6 at 20 °C, R2 = 15 at 35 °C, so T2 − T1 = 15.
  2. Q10. (15/6)10/15 = 2.50.667 ≈ 1.84.
  3. Rate at 25 °C. That is 5 °C above 20, so multiply by 1.845/10 = 1.36: 6 × 1.36 ≈ 8.1 µmol/min.

Common mistakes

  • Using the temperature difference in the exponent as 1 instead of 10/ΔT.
  • Forgetting to square-root or cube-root fractional exponents when ΔT is not 10.
  • Applying Q10 above the temperature where enzymes denature.
  • In serial dilutions, using the factor once instead of once per tube.
  • Comparing rates measured over different time intervals.

Reading rates from graphs

For a straight section of a quantity-time graph, rate is rise over run with units. If the graph curves, take the slope of the tangent at the point asked (an initial rate is the slope near t = 0). Always keep the units, for example mL O2 per minute.
