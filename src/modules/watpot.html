Water potential

Water moves toward lower water potential. The number tells you the direction and, when you compare two places, which way the cell will swell or shrink.

The idea

Water potential (Ψ) measures the tendency of water to move. Water always moves from higher to lower Ψ. It has two parts: solute potential Ψs = −iCRT (always zero or negative; more solute means lower) and pressure potential Ψp (positive in a turgid plant cell, zero in an open beaker). Ψ = Ψp + Ψs. Pure water in an open container has Ψ = 0.

Here i is the ionization constant (1 for sucrose, 2 for NaCl), C is molar concentration, R = 0.0831 L·bar/(mol·K) and T is in kelvin (°C + 273). The result is in bars. A plant cell in pure water takes in water until Ψp rises enough to make the cell Ψ equal to 0, so no net movement: turgor.

Try this

Predict what will happen first. Then set it up and check.

  1. Cell solute 0.4 M, pressure 0, beaker 0.1 M sucrose. Where does water go?

    What you should see

    Cell Ψ = −9.9 bar; beaker Ψ = −2.5 bar. Water moves into the cell (toward lower Ψ).

  2. Raise the cell pressure until the cell Ψ equals the beaker Ψ. What is the pressure?

    What you should see

    About 7.4 bar (−9.9 + 7.4 = −2.5). Water stops entering: the cell is turgid.

Worked example

A plant cell has solute concentration 0.3 M sucrose and Ψp = 0 at 25 °C. It is placed in 0.1 M sucrose. Find each Ψ and the direction of water movement.

Try it yourself first, then reveal one step at a time.

  1. Constants. R = 0.0831, T = 298 K, RT ≈ 24.8 L·bar/mol; i = 1.
  2. Cell. Ψs = −(1)(0.3)(24.8) = −7.4 bar; Ψ = 0 + (−7.4) = −7.4 bar.
  3. Beaker. Ψs = −(1)(0.1)(24.8) = −2.5 bar; open so Ψp = 0 and Ψ = −2.5 bar.
  4. Direction. Water moves from −2.5 (higher) to −7.4 (lower): into the cell.

Common mistakes

  • Forgetting the negative sign on Ψs.
  • Using °C instead of kelvin.
  • Saying water moves toward higher Ψ.
  • Forgetting i for ionic solutes such as NaCl.
  • Thinking pressure potential is always zero. It is zero in an open container, positive in a turgid cell.

What the FRQ asks

Most questions give concentrations, ask for Ψ of each side, then ask which way water moves and what happens to the cell. Show the substitution with units, state the direction in words, and for plant cells mention the role of Ψp (turgor).
