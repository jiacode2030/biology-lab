ATP & coupled reactions

Cells run on one common energy currency. Many reactions that would not go on their own are driven by splitting ATP.

The idea

ATP (adenosine triphosphate) has an adenine base, a ribose sugar and three phosphate groups. The phosphate groups are negatively charged and repel one another, so the bonds between them hold energy. Hydrolysis of the terminal phosphate (ATP + H₂O → ADP + Pi) releases about 30.5 kJ/mol under standard conditions.

Cells use this by energy coupling: an unfavorable (endergonic) reaction is linked to ATP hydrolysis, often by transferring the phosphate onto the reactant, so the combined ΔG is negative. ATP is then rebuilt from ADP + Pi using energy from respiration or photosynthesis. A cell turns over its ATP supply constantly.

Try this

Predict what will happen first. Then set it up and check.

  1. Set the reaction to need +20 kJ/mol. Does one ATP make it go?

    What you should see

    Yes: −30.5 + 20 = −10.5 kJ/mol, which is negative, so the coupled reaction is favorable.

  2. Set +45 kJ/mol. How many ATP are needed?

    What you should see

    One ATP gives net +14.5 (not favorable). Two give −16 kJ/mol: favorable. Two ATP are needed.

Worked example

The synthesis of glutamine from glutamate and ammonia has ΔG = +14 kJ/mol. When coupled to ATP hydrolysis (−30.5 kJ/mol), find the net ΔG and decide if it is favorable.

Try it yourself first, then reveal one step at a time.

  1. Add. +14 + (−30.5) = −16.5 kJ/mol.
  2. Decide. Net ΔG is negative, so the coupled process is favorable.
  3. How. The phosphate is transferred to glutamate first, making it reactive enough to combine with ammonia.

Common mistakes

  • Saying the energy is stored in the bonds as if breaking a bond releases energy. The release comes from forming more stable products.
  • Thinking ATP is a long-term energy store. It is short term; fats and glycogen are storage.
  • Forgetting that net ΔG must be negative for the coupled reaction to go.
  • Mixing up ADP and ATP.
  • Ignoring that ATP must be re-made.

Writing the explanation

A good answer states the reaction is endergonic, that ATP hydrolysis is exergonic, that the two are coupled (often by phosphorylation) so net ΔG is negative, and that cellular respiration or photosynthesis regenerates ATP.
