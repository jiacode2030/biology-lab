Fermentation & anaerobic respiration

Without oxygen the electron transport chain stops, but glycolysis can keep running if the cell can recycle its NADH.

The idea

Glycolysis needs NAD⁺ to keep going. When oxygen is absent, the chain cannot re-oxidise NADH to NAD⁺. Fermentation solves this by using pyruvate (or a product of it) as the electron acceptor so NAD⁺ is regenerated. In yeast, pyruvate becomes ethanol and CO₂ (alcoholic fermentation). In animal muscle, pyruvate becomes lactate (lactic acid fermentation).

Fermentation makes only the 2 ATP of glycolysis per glucose, compared with about 30–32 with oxygen. So a fermenting cell must use about fifteen times as much glucose for the same ATP. Some prokaryotes carry out anaerobic respiration with an acceptor other than O₂ at the end of an electron transport chain.

Try this

Predict what will happen first. Then set it up and check.

  1. Yeast with no oxygen and 60 ATP needed. How much glucose?

    What you should see

    60 ATP ÷ 2 ATP per glucose = 30 glucose.

  2. Now add oxygen (100). How does the glucose needed change?

    What you should see

    About 60/30 = 2 glucose: respiration with oxygen is roughly fifteen times as efficient.

Worked example

A muscle cell needs 90 ATP. Compare the glucose needed during hard exercise (lactic fermentation, 2 ATP per glucose) with rest (aerobic, about 30 ATP per glucose).

Try it yourself first, then reveal one step at a time.

  1. Fermentation. 90 / 2 = 45 glucose.
  2. Aerobic. 90 / 30 = 3 glucose.
  3. Conclusion. Fermentation uses 15 times as much glucose, and the lactate (and acidity) it builds up contributes to fatigue.

Common mistakes

  • Saying fermentation makes no ATP. It makes 2 per glucose via glycolysis.
  • Saying fermentation produces NADH. It consumes it, regenerating NAD⁺.
  • Thinking muscles make ethanol or yeast makes lactate.
  • Mixing fermentation (no electron transport chain) with anaerobic respiration (other final acceptor).
  • Forgetting that pyruvate is the starting point of both aerobic and anaerobic pathways.

Why it matters

Fermentation lets cells survive without oxygen for a while. Its purpose is to regenerate NAD⁺ so glycolysis continues; the ATP is what keeps the cell alive. If asked why the cell does not just stop, answer in terms of NAD⁺.
